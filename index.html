Introduction

More recently, hybrid powertrains have become common in high-end sports cars and racing. Modern Formula 1 cars, for instance, use 1.6L V6 turbocharged engines combined with electric systems, yet they still produce over 1,000 horsepower formula1.com. Similarly, hypercars like the McLaren P1 combine a twin-turbo V8 with an electric motor. The P1’s electric boost adds 179 PS – more than twice the power of an F1 car’s KERS unit– to achieve a total output around 916 PS . Scholars note that the move to hybrids reflects a shift toward better efficiency and sustainability even in racing-focused cars . Overall, the literature indicates engines have evolved from “big and thirsty” to “small and supercharged,” and now to “integrated hybrid” units, balancing raw performance with fuel savings and lower emissions..
